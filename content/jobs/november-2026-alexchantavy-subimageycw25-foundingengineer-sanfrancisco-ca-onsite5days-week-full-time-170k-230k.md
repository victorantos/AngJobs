Subimage (YC W25) - Founding Engineer - San Francisco, CA - ONSITE (5 days&#x2F;week) - Full-time - $170K–$230K + 0.5%–1.0% equity - <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;subimage.io">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;subimage.io</a>

Hihi, I&#x27;m Alex, co-founder and CEO. Subimage maps infrastructure the way an attacker does so security teams at scale-ups and enterprises can find and fix problems. We&#x27;re built on Cartography (<a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;cartography.dev" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;cartography.dev</a>), the open source infra mapping tool we helped create at Lyft and donated to the CNCF. It runs at over 70 companies, including 7 in the Fortune 100.

We&#x27;re a tiny seed-stage team and our customers are companies your parents have heard of. We have more demand than we can build for, which is why this post exists. This is a chance to get in on the ground floor of something big (or to find out quickly that it won&#x27;t work; just being upfront about it).

Some of the problems you&#x27;d be working on:

- Vulnerability triage with real context: is the asset internet-facing, through which services, is the vulnerable function actually reachable in code, are there compensating controls, and what sensitive data does it reach once exploited, across how many hops.

- Proving that the security fixes our agent proposes won&#x27;t break anything.

- A near-real-time, self-updating map of an environment so you can see an attack as it happens, plus &quot;time travel&quot; in the graph so you can replay it afterward.

- Teaching an agent to answer questions about a large graph quickly, with only the context it needs and without making mistakes.

- Determining who owns a given resource from messy signals like tags, actions, logs, and files.

Stack: Cartography for ingestion, Neo4j for the graph, Python&#x2F;FastAPI backend, Svelte frontend with a homegrown WebGL graph renderer, an MCP server that exposes all of it to agents, Terraform for IaC.

Who we&#x27;re looking for: 3+ years at high-growth companies, strong distributed systems and cloud experience, a real sense of ownership, first-principles thinking, and a willingness to implement gnarly business logic (elegantly, ideally) if it makes a jaded security engineer&#x27;s life easier. Kubernetes and security knowledge are nice to have, not required. You&#x27;re in the Bay Area and want to be in the office five days a week.

Process: technical screen, call with the founders, a paid day working with the team, then offer. Health&#x2F;vision&#x2F;dental, 401k match, gym, team lunches, unlimited PTO.
